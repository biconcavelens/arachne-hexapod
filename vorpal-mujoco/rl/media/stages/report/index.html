Arachne hexapod · fault-tolerant path following · simulation · 20 Nov 2026

Four environment stages: results

MuJoCo MJX + PPO. One policy per stage and sensor set, 40M training steps each, run locally on an RTX 4060. Nothing here is from the real robot.

What we found

  1. The robot keeps to the drawn path when servos fail. With one broken servo it stays 3–7 mm from the line on average in every stage, at 84–97% of healthy speed. Two dead servos still reach the goal 86–95% of the time when nothing moves in the scene.
  2. Training on rough ground matters. On the terrain, the terrain-trained policy reaches the goal in 100% of single-fault episodes and 91% with three mixed faults; the room-trained policy manages 82% and 66%.
  3. IMU only is as good as IMU + camera, or better, everywhere. It also tracks the path more tightly. At 24×16 pixels the camera adds little; this is the case for the higher-resolution, optical-flow run on a cloud GPU.
  4. Moving objects are the weak point. Policies trained with movers do no better than ones that never saw them, and the robot is still hit in 17–56% of episodes. They have not learned to wait.
  5. Naming the broken servo works only from the IMU in the room (right servo 56% of the time, chance is 8%). On terrain, from the full observation, it is near chance.

The four stages

Stage 1
Furnished room

6×6 m room, random furniture placed off the path, flat floor.

Stage 2
Room + movers

Robot vacuum, two rolling balls and a walking person cross the path.

Stage 3
Rough terrain

8×8 m field of hills, bumps and stones at 3 cm resolution; rocks, logs, trees.

Stage 4
Terrain + movers

The field plus a dog, a football, a hiker and a robot lawnmower.

In every stage a path of about 1 m is drawn from the top-down view, and the robot must walk along it to the goal. Faults are random: zero to three servos, each dead (limp), stuck (frozen in place), signal loss (commands randomly dropped; the servo holds the last one) or worn gears (backlash in the gearbox), starting either before the walk or partway through. The policy is told nothing about the fault. Speed losses up to 50% are not penalised; cross-track error is.

Each policy sees one of three sensor sets: IMU only, camera only (the 24×16 grey body camera, three frames), or both. No sensor noise yet. Movers that can stop (vacuum, person, dog, hiker, mower) wait while the robot is in their way. Walking into a mover ends the episode; being hit from the side or behind counts as "struck" but does not.

Does training in the harder stage help?

Each block tests several policies in one stage. Success rate (%) with the mean distance from the path (mm) below it; the stage's own policy is first.

Terrain: yes, clearly. The room policy is 12–27 points behind once servos fail, and 7 behind even when healthy.

Movers: no. In both mover stages, the policy trained with movers is within a few points of one trained without them. The "struck" column barely moves either. The penalty for walking into a mover is the same size as for a fall, and the policy seems to have traded it off against reaching the goal rather than learned to wait.

Faults in the room: a policy trained only healthy (room_both_healthy) does as well under faults as the fault-trained one in the flat room. Fault training pays off only on rough ground, where the gait has less margin.

Naming the broken servo

A separate network reads the policy's inputs over the last few seconds and outputs, for each of the 12 servos, the probability that it is broken. Tested on 308 held-out episodes per policy.

With the IMU alone it finds the broken servo more than half the time, within about 3 s. Once the 1,700 camera numbers are in its input it does much worse, and on terrain, where ground bumps shake the body much like a fault does, it is close to chance. The next step is to diagnose from the IMU and the servo commands only, whatever the policy itself sees.

Next

  1. Movers: raise the cost of walking into one, give the actor the mover's apparent motion in the camera, and add an explicit reward for waiting.
  2. Diagnosis: IMU + commands only, trained per stage.
  3. Camera: cloud GPU run with a higher-resolution body camera and an optical-flow input, then sensor noise.
  4. Interactive sim: load the new terrain policies so a path can be drawn and servos killed live.
