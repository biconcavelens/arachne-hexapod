# Camera cap

The Vorpal cap with a slide-in mount for a USB camera board on its knob, and a cable
slot through the dome behind the knob. Generated by `make_camera_cap.py` from the
original cap (`Hexapod_V1r8a_Cap_VARIATION_NoSkeletonize.stl`, Vorpal, CC BY-SA, see
`NOTICE.md`).

| File | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `cap_with_camera_mount.stl` | Print this instead of the original cap. Rim down on the bed, like the original. |
| `camera_mount_only.stl` | Or glue just the mount onto the knob of the cap you already have. |
| `camera_pose.json` | Where the lens ends up (base frame), read by `fault-sim` to place the simulated camera. |
| `preview.png` | Side and front views. |

## How it holds the camera

The board (30 x 25 mm) slides in from the top between two C-shaped rails. Each rail grips
2 mm of the board's side edge, front and back. The rest of the front stays open for the
lens and the back stays open for the cable, which runs back and down through the slot in
the dome. The rails lean forward so the camera looks 20 degrees below horizontal. A flared
pedestal lifts the rails 8 mm above the knob; its sides are under 35 degrees, so it prints
without supports.

## Check before printing

- **Board thickness.** I assumed 1.6 mm (a standard PCB) plus 0.4 mm clearance, so the slot
  gap is 2.0 mm. Measure your board at the edges and set `BOARD_T`.
- **Parts near the edges.** The rails cover 2 mm of each side edge, front and back. If the
  board has components or the lens holder within 2 mm of a side edge, reduce `GRIP`.
- **Lens field of view.** The 20 degree tilt keeps the legs out of view for a camera with up
  to about 45 degrees vertical field of view (my estimate from the leg geometry; the
  simulator's leg-visibility check confirms it once it is run). A wider lens may need less tilt.
- **Cable.** The slot is 7 x 12 mm. If your cable's plug is bigger, thread the cable
  before plugging it in, or widen `CABLE_SLOT`.
- **Cap height.** The original cap STL comes in the same frame as the base, so I placed the
  camera from it. On the real robot, measure the lens height above the floor while standing
  and compare with about 197 mm (95 mm body height plus 102 mm lens height above the base origin).

Change any number at the top of `make_camera_cap.py` and run it again (a few seconds;
needs `trimesh` and `manifold3d`, plus the original Vorpal download in `Vorpal needed/`).
